What people build with Formulize

Most of it starts the same way: a spreadsheet that three people are emailing back and forth, and nobody is sure which copy is current.

Formulize replaces that with forms other people can fill in, rules about who sees what, and reports that are always up to date. You build it by clicking, not by coding — which means you can change it next month when the work changes.

Six things it is good at

These are illustrative examples, not case studies — a sketch of how each one is put together, so you can see whether your own situation looks like any of them.

Funding

Grant applications and reporting

Applications arrive by email, in different formats, and the reporting deadlines creep up on you.

  • One form for the application, one for each progress report
  • The two joined, so every report sits under its grant
  • Applicants see only their own entries; staff see all of them
  • A report of what is outstanding, filtered by date
People

Volunteer and shift scheduling

You have more volunteers than you can keep in your head, and no reliable picture of who is covering what.

  • A volunteer form and a shift form, joined together
  • Volunteers sign themselves up and update their own details
  • Coordinators see the whole schedule, volunteers see theirs
  • Search for shifts with nobody assigned to them
Membership

Memberships and renewals

The membership list lives in a spreadsheet, and renewal dates are tracked by whoever remembers to look.

  • A member form, with a renewals form joined to it
  • Import the spreadsheet you already have to start
  • Members update their own record; nobody else sees it
  • A standing report of who is lapsing in the next month
Casework

Client intake and case notes

Intake is on paper, notes are in personal folders, and handover between staff loses things.

  • An intake form, with case notes joined to each client
  • Entries owned by the team that created them
  • Front-desk staff see intake, caseworkers see the notes
  • Export to Excel when a funder wants numbers
Resources

Equipment and room bookings

Bookings happen by asking whoever has the calendar, and two people turn up for the same room.

  • An items form and a bookings form, joined together
  • Anyone can request; a named group approves
  • A shared list of what is out and when it is due back
  • Filter by this month, or the last twelve
Events

Event registration and RSVPs

Registrations come in by email and someone retypes them into a list for the door.

  • A public form anyone can fill in, no account needed
  • An events form joined to it, so each event has its own list
  • Organisers see everything; the public form shows nothing back
  • A door list you can sort and search on the day

The same idea, in every one

Every example above is the same three moves. Once you have seen them done once, the rest is variations.

Make the forms

  • One form per kind of thing you are tracking
  • Any number of forms, any number of entries
  • Or describe it to an AI assistant and let it build them

Join them together

  • Each client has many notes; each grant has many reports
  • The connection is a setting, not code
  • Reports can then draw on both forms at once

Decide who sees what

  • Per group, per form, and per entry
  • People can be limited to only their own entries
  • Searchable reports published to some or all of them
