Why do people use Formulize?

To move beyond cobbled together solutions

  • A monster spreadsheet that breaks whenever someone edits the wrong cell.

To get away from the square peg, round hole problem

  • No off-the-shelf product fits the way you actually work.

To eliminate data sovereignty concerns

  • Your data is locked away in systems you don't control.

What do people build with Formulize?

People and relationships

Memberships and renewals

The membership list lives in a spreadsheet, and renewal dates are tracked by whoever remembers to look.

  • A member form, with a renewals form joined to it
  • Import the spreadsheet you already have to start
  • Members update their own record; nobody else sees it
  • A standing report of who is lapsing in the next month

Contact management and CRM

Contacts are split across email, spreadsheets, and someone's phone, and nobody has the full picture.

  • A contacts form, with interactions or notes joined to each one
  • Everyone works from the same record, updated in one place
  • Segment contacts by group, tag, or status
  • A report of who hasn't been followed up with

Volunteer and shift scheduling

You have more volunteers than you can keep in your head, and no reliable picture of who is covering what.

  • A volunteer form and a shift form, joined together
  • Volunteers sign themselves up and update their own details
  • Coordinators see the whole schedule, volunteers see theirs
  • Search for shifts with nobody assigned to them

Managing placements and co-ops

Matching people to placements is a spreadsheet, a lot of email, and a process that's a little different every term.

  • A candidates form and a placements form, joined together
  • Track preferences, capacity, and status as matches are made
  • Coordinators see everything; participants see only their own match
  • Adjust the process whenever the rules change, without starting over

Money and resources

Grant applications and reporting

Applications arrive by email, in different formats, and the reporting deadlines creep up on you.

  • One form for the application, one for each progress report
  • The two joined, so every report sits under its grant
  • Applicants see only their own entries; staff see all of them
  • A report of what is outstanding, filtered by date

Expense claims and approvals

Receipts come in by email, approvals happen somewhere in an inbox, and nobody can say what is still owed.

  • A claims form, with an approval step joined to it
  • Staff submit their own; approvers see only what needs their sign-off
  • A running report of what is approved but not yet paid
  • Export the totals to Excel when it is time to reconcile

Equipment and room bookings

Bookings happen by asking whoever has the calendar, and two people turn up for the same room.

  • An items form and a bookings form, joined together
  • Anyone can request; a named group approves
  • A shared list of what is out and when it is due back
  • Filter by this month, or the last twelve

Inventory and equipment tracking

The stock list lives in someone's head, or a spreadsheet that is already out of date.

  • An items form, with a movements form joined to it
  • Anyone logs what came in or went out; one report shows what is on hand
  • Search by location, category, or condition
  • Filter for what needs reordering, right now

Operations and casework

Client intake and case notes

Intake is on paper, notes are in personal folders, and handover between staff loses things.

  • An intake form, with case notes joined to each client
  • Entries owned by the team that created them
  • Front-desk staff see intake, caseworkers see the notes
  • Export to Excel when a funder wants numbers

Activity logging

Work gets done, but there's no record of it beyond whoever happens to remember doing it.

  • A log form anyone on the team can fill in, in seconds
  • Entries linked to the client, project, or case they belong to
  • A running history nobody has to reconstruct from memory
  • Filter by person, date, or type of activity

Time tracking

Timesheets are a spreadsheet per person, and totalling them up is a monthly chore.

  • A time entry form, joined to the project or client it is billed to
  • People log their own hours; managers see everyone's
  • Totals reported by person, project, or date range
  • Export the totals for payroll or billing

Assessments and evaluations

Every round of assessments is its own spreadsheet, and pulling the results together takes days.

  • One assessment form, joined to the person or program it covers
  • Assessors see only what is assigned to them
  • Results compared and reported across everyone at once
  • Reuse the same form next round, changed or not

Public forms and content

Event registration and RSVPs

Registrations come in by email and someone retypes them into a list for the door.

  • A public form anyone can fill in, no account needed
  • An events form joined to it, so each event has its own list
  • Organisers see everything; the public form shows nothing back
  • A door list you can sort and search on the day

Surveys and questionnaires

A survey tool works fine until you need the results joined to the rest of your data.

  • A public form for respondents, no account required
  • Responses joined to the person, event, or program they relate to
  • Results reported alongside everything else you track
  • Reuse or adapt the same form for the next round

Headless content management

You want a custom front end, but still need somewhere structured to manage the content behind it.

  • Formulize as the backend; any front end consumes it over the API
  • This site's own News section is a live example
  • Editors work in ordinary forms; nothing about the API changes when the content does
  • No separate CMS to license, host, or keep in sync

Do any of those sound familiar?

Formulize has helped organizations with all these needs. Memberships might seem different from Activity Logs, but to a database it's all variations on a theme.

Make the forms

  • One form per kind of thing you are tracking
  • Create as many forms as you need, each with any number of entries
  • Or describe them to an AI assistant and let it build them for you

Join them together

  • Each client has many notes; each grant has many reports
  • The connection is a setting, not code
  • Reports can then draw on both forms at once

Decide who sees what

  • Control who sees which entries in which forms
  • Restrict who can create and modify data
  • Publish searchable reports to your users

Formulize gives you complete control, by letting you build solutions that meet your needs, precisely. You build by clicking, not by coding, so whenever the goalposts move again, you just change one setting and the whole application updates automatically.
